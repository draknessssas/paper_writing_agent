# IEEE Editorial Conventions (venue-neutral)

Scope: house style shared by IEEE Transactions, Journals, and Letters, taken from the IEEE Editorial Style Manual for Authors and the IEEE Author Center. Venue-specific rules (page limits, fees, scope statements, review mechanics) do not belong here; they live in `knowledge/venues/<venue>.md` and are loaded only when `profiles/active_profile.yml` names a venue.

As of 2026-07. Verify against the current IEEE Editorial Style Manual before relying on a detail that decides acceptance.

## 1. Article section order

1. Title page: title, byline, membership, first footnote (funding goes in the first footnote, not the Acknowledgment; ORCID for the corresponding author)
2. Abstract (one paragraph)
3. Index Terms
4. Nomenclature (optional)
5. Introduction
6. Body
7. Conclusion
8. Appendix(es)
9. Acknowledgment
10. References
11. Photographs and Biographies (when the venue requires them)

Titles: avoid extraneous words such as "new" or "novel"; they carry no search value and invite the reviewer to test the claim. A first-page footnote must disclose any prior conference presentation (name, dates, location) or related submissions; the conference paper must also appear in the references.

## 2. Abstract, index terms, nomenclature

- Abstract: one paragraph, self-contained, 150 to 250 words per the IEEE-wide manual (many Transactions narrow this to 150 to 200; check the venue file). No abbreviations, footnotes, references, displayed equations, or tabular material. Summarize the conclusions, not the plan.
- Index Terms: mandatory, author-provided, alphabetical, placed as the final paragraph of the Abstract section; capitalize only the first word unless capitalized in text; include acronym definitions followed by the acronym in parentheses.
- Nomenclature: optional; follows Abstract and Index Terms and precedes the Introduction; unnumbered primary heading; each item ends with a period; items must not begin with "is" or "the"; no equality symbols between the two columns; acronyms defined in the Nomenclature need not be redefined in text.

## 3. Writing conventions

- Variables italic; vectors boldface italic; roman units with a regular space between number and unit (400 V, 100 kHz, 0.2 T).
- Numerals always with units; zero before decimals (0.25); thin spaces instead of commas in numbers of five or more digits (62 000); ranges repeat no unit unless the units differ (40–50 mm); compound units with a center dot or slash (V/s); percentages always use the % sign; hyphenate units as compound adjectives only when needed for clarity (a 10-kV bus, but a current of 2 A).
- Lowercase "dc" and "ac" in running text ("DC" only at the start of a sentence or in a title); en dash for numeric ranges and opposites (voltage–current curve, dc–dc converter). No em dashes in prose.
- Captions: table number and title centered above the table (label TABLE, Roman numerals), table caption text takes no terminal period. Figure captions go below figures; capitalize the first word; never begin a caption with "A", "An", or "The". Subfigures: "(a) ... (b) ...".
- American spelling per Merriam-Webster; no contractions; serial comma; "data" is plural; dates as 4 June 2002; write "in Section II-A", never "Subsection".
- Acronyms defined at first use in the Abstract and again in the body; since most Transactions abstracts must carry no abbreviations, prefer spelled-out terms there. Define each acronym exactly once in the body.
- Citations: numbers in square brackets; write "in [1]", never "in reference [1]"; cross-reference as [1, Fig. 2] or [1, eq. (8)]; one reference per number; ranges as [3]–[6]; "Fig." even at sentence start; "Equation" only at sentence start, otherwise just "(1)".
- Journal abbreviations in references follow the official IEEE list (for example `IEEE Trans. Power Electron.`, `IEEE Trans. Ind. Electron.`, `IEEE J. Emerg. Sel. Topics Power Electron.`). Do not invent abbreviations; look them up.
- "This article" vs "this paper": the current IEEE style manual says "article"; many venues still say "paper". Either is acceptable; stay consistent within one manuscript and register the choice in the terminology registry.
- Third person; past tense for what was measured or done in this work, present tense for what figures show and for established facts.

## 4. Figures and tables

- Minimum 300 dpi; all lettering at least 8 pt at final printed size, with fonts consistent with the text (a resistor "R" in text must not be "r" in the figure).
- Single-column width 3.5 in (8.9 cm); maximum two-column width 7 1/16 in (17.8 cm).
- Print is black-and-white by default at most venues; figures must remain readable in grayscale, and text must never refer to figure elements by color alone.
- Every figure and table is cited in the text before it appears, and the text interprets it ("Fig. 3 shows X under Y, which confirms Z"), it does not merely point ("see Fig. 3").
- Measured-result captions state the operating or evaluation condition; comparison figures and tables state the normalization conditions.

## 5. AI-content disclosure

IEEE policy (Editorial Style Manual, updated 29 July 2024): the use of AI-generated content (text, figures, images, code) shall be disclosed in the Acknowledgments section of any article submitted to an IEEE publication, identifying the AI system and the specific sections that used it, with a brief explanation. AI used for editing and grammar enhancement is common practice and generally outside the intent of the policy, but disclosure is recommended. This workspace drafts manuscript text: plan the Acknowledgment disclosure with the user before submission.

## 6. Common rejection grounds across IEEE Transactions

Stated in most Transactions' author guidelines and worth checking before submission: inadequate English; inadequate literature review or references; insufficient validation of the main claims; contribution outside the venue's scope; similarity-check failure. Reviewers are not expected to correct language.
